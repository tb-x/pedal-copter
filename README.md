# Pedal-Copter

A sky-taxi game for young kids, inspired by the old game Ugh!. A caveman pedals a wooden helicopter with a rotor
of big leaves around a stone-age valley. Cave people come out of their caves, wait on the landing stones and show
a bubble with the stop they want (its colour, a little picture and a big number). Pick them up and fly them there.

It has twelve levels and two ways to fly, and works offline from the Home Screen. Everything is drawn in code (no
picture files); the voices, the music and most sound effects are recorded, and the rotor and a few small sounds are
made in the browser.

- 🏠 in the corner goes back to the list of all games.

**Play:** https://tb-x.github.io/pedal-copter/ (once published; it isn't yet).

## What you see

The valley is drawn like a museum diorama, with the front of the rock cut away to show its layers (sandstone,
limestone, little fossil shells). Everything is built in code; there are no picture files.

- **Caves** are rooms cut open at the front, each with ochre paintings on the walls (mammoths and hunters, fruit
  trees, fish, flames) and someone at home: a cave person stirring a pot by the fire with a baby asleep on a fur bed
  (homes), a kid munching an apple next to baskets of fruit (fruit stop), someone waving a fish under a drying rack
  (fishing stop), a cook turning meat on a spit (campfire stop). People waiting walk out of the tunnel at the back.
  Later stops add a painting cave (a kid painting the wall, pots of paint), a hot spring (someone soaking in a
  steaming pool), a wheel workshop (stone wheels and a worker with a hammer) and a dino nest (spotted eggs, one
  wobbling, and a kid watching it).
- **Stops** have a round landing stone with a ring in their colour, a carved plaque with their number and picture,
  and a torch.
- **The copter** is a woven basket with a rounded bow on curled log skids, rails lashed with vines, a stone pedal
  wheel in the bow with pedals on both sides, a vine belt up to the mast, a rotor of four big leaves right above the
  middle, a tail boom with a hide fin and a little leaf rotor, fur cushions, a gourd lamp per seat and a torch that
  lights up the ground after dark. It's a real 3D model: when it changes direction it swings round (through facing
  you) and banks into the turn, dips its nose when flying forward, and the pilot and riders keep facing you. The
  pilot has a beard and a bone in his hair.
- **People** wear spotted furs with a strap, a bead necklace with a tooth, and all sorts of hair (buns, spikes, long
  hair, a bone tuft). Some are kids.
- **Around them**: grass tufts, flowers, pebbles, ferns, roots hanging down the rock, palm and apple trees; behind
  the back wall a plateau with palms, hills, a smoking volcano and drifting clouds.
- **Animals**: the dinosaurs have spotted, scaly skin, knees and feet, and tails and necks made of segments that
  bend. A little T-rex runs after a butterfly (level 1), legs and tail swinging, arms waving, turning round when the
  butterfly does, snapping at it and roaring now and then (when you hear the roar). A long-neck strolls behind the
  wall (level 2 and others) on four walking legs, swaying its neck, dipping it to graze and turning round slowly at
  the ends. Another sleeps in the valley on level 6, breathing, its tail twitching. Pterodactyls beat their wings
  from the shoulder and fold them at the wrist, and glide now and then. There's also a mammoth and her baby, birds,
  a fish jumping in the lake and dragonflies.
- **Time and weather**: day, dusk (sunset, fireflies), night (moon, stars, glowing caves, torches and the copter's
  torch), rain (grey sky, rain streaks) and snow (white ground, snowy pines, falling flakes). Windy levels have leaves
  blowing across. The last level has the volcano close up, erupting, with glowing lava streams.

## How it plays

- People walk out of a cave and wait beside a landing stone, with a bubble showing where they want to go.
- Land on their stone and they hop in by themselves. Land on the stop in their bubble and they hop out, cheer and go
  into that cave.
- The lamps on the copter show which seats are taken. When it's full, the lamps flash and the next person waits.
- Bumping into rock just bounces the copter back (the pilot's eyes spin). Dipping into the lake splashes it back up.
- There's plenty of sky: the copter can climb well above the top of the picture, and the camera rises with it.
- Faces go from happy to grumpy to red the longer people wait. Waiting in the copter is much less annoying. A red,
  cross person stomps back into their cave (counted next to the walking man at the top).
- Each level needs a number of happy arrivals. Three stars if nobody gave up, two if one or two did, otherwise one.
  No timer and no losing.
- **Twelve levels**, each adding something:
  1. Two Caves: two stops, one seat, tap a stop to fly there, the hint hand helps.
  2. Fruit Tree: three stops.
  3. The River: dusk, two seats, a lake to splash in and a waterfall.
  4. Tall Cliff: stops at very different heights; on a phone the view scrolls up and down.
  5. Windy Day: the wind pushes the copter to the right in gusts (let go and it drifts).
  6. Dino Meadow: three seats; a big long-neck sleeps in the middle and you can land on its back; a pterodactyl
     flies through now and then.
  7. Dusk at the Lake: the pilot gets hungry (see below); at "fishing time" a group all want the lake.
  8. Wide Valley: two screens wide; the camera follows the copter and arrows at the edge point to people waiting
     off-screen and to where riders want to go.
  9. Torch Night: night; a stop's torch only burns when someone waits there or a rider is going there.
  10. Rainy Day: rain, a big waterfall, and grandmas (they walk slowly but wait patiently).
  11. Ice Age: snow and a little wind, a four-seat copter with a hide roof, and hunters carrying a huge fish (they
      take two seats).
  12. Volcano Feast: the volcano erupts behind, smoke rings drift by to fly through (sparkles and a chime, just for
      fun), and lots of people head for the campfire feast.
- **Hunger** (levels 7–12): a drumstick meter at the top empties slowly while flying, faster when pedalling hard.
  Land on a fruit or campfire stop and the pilot munches it full again. Low, and the meter pulses, a 🍗 bubble pops
  up over the pilot and the hint hand points at food. Empty, and the copter still flies but slowly, so nobody is ever
  stuck.
- **Pterodactyls** (levels 6–8, 12) fly across high up. Bumping one is never a crash: the copter is nudged aside,
  the pilot's eyes spin and the pterodactyl squawks and flaps away.

## Controls

Pick the way to fly on the level map (the two buttons at the bottom):

- 👆 **Follow my finger** (default): touch and hold anywhere and the copter flies towards your finger. Let go and it
  slowly comes down. Coming down near a landing stone settles it onto the stone.
- 🕹️ **Pedal and steer**: hold the big foot button to pedal (climb), ◀ ▶ to fly sideways. Let go of the foot and the
  copter sinks.
- On levels 1 and 2, a short tap on a stop flies the copter there by itself.
- Keyboard: arrow keys or WASD, Space to pedal.

## Play offline (add to Home Screen)

On iPhone or iPad, open the game in Safari, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**, then open it once from the new icon
while online. After that it starts full screen and works without internet. Changes arrive on their own: the next
online launch downloads them and the one after shows them.

(How: `manifest.webmanifest` gives the icon and full-screen mode; `sw.js`, a service worker copied from Blast Off,
keeps a copy of every file the game uses, including three.js and the font. The icons `icon-180.png` and
`icon-512.png` are rendered from `icon.svg` in headless Chrome. On this computer the service worker asks the server
first, so edits show on the next reload.)

## Running it

Serve the folder and open it in a browser:

```bash
python -m http.server 5229
```

Then open http://localhost:5229. Test switches on the address: `?level=N` (1-12), `?ctl=follow` or `?ctl=pads`,
`?demo` (the autopilot plays), `?fast` (4× speed), `?mute` (no sound at all), `?seed=N`, `?tick` (timer ticks for
background tabs), `?unlock` (all levels open), `?tapfly` (tap-to-fly on every level), `?time=day|dusk|night` (any
level at any time of day), `?look=x,y,distance` (a fixed close-up camera for checking the art, e.g.
`?look=4.9,4.5,9`). `window.__game.G` holds the state, `window.__game.renderer.info` the draw cost, and
`<html data-phase>` says which screen is showing.

**Speed.** Most of the time a frame is about 80–230 draw calls, up to about 350 at the busiest moments of the last
levels. To stay smooth on phones: the pixel density is capped (1.5× on phones, 2× elsewhere) and steps down by
itself when frames run slow and back up when there's room (as in Going Up!), phones use simpler shadow edges, the sun
shadow is redrawn every other frame, the clouds are one instanced draw, and still scenery is merged into one mesh
per material when a level starts. Tuning: `MAX_DPR_PHONE`, `MAX_DPR`, `DPR_MIN`, `SLOW_FRAME_MS`, `FAST_FRAME_MS`,
`SHADOW_EVERY`.

**Telemetry.** Played through the laptop's root server on port 5200 (`_telemetry/serve.py`), the game reports
errors, sessions, `level-start`, `level-end` (stars, give-ups, bumps, time) and `gave-up`, plus frame times and draw
calls (`window.__tbxRenderer`). To check a phone or tablet, play at `http://<laptop-ip>:5200/pedal-copter/` and
then run `python _telemetry/report.py pedal-copter`. Nothing is sent from the published game.

## Sound

All synthesized in the browser; there are no sound files.

- **The copter**: a buzzy rotor that goes "whup-whup" as the four leaves pass, louder and higher when pedalling hard,
  and a wooden creak on each pedal stroke.
- **Effects**: lift-off, a thump on landing, a boing for bumps, a splash, hop-in chirps and a hop-out with a cheer,
  a grumble and three stomps when someone gives up, the "full" bleep, munching, a tummy rumble when food runs low,
  a pterodactyl squawk, a chime for flying through a smoke ring, stars and a fanfare.
- **Music**: three recorded stone-age loops (see Audio credits): a bouncy marimba-and-log-drum one for day, dusk and
  snow levels, a calm one for night and rain, and a fast campfire-party one for the volcano feast. Each is made
  seamless when it loads (a quiet tail trimmed, its last 2 s blended into its start). It dips while someone speaks
  or the fanfare plays, and stops on the level map. Until a loop has loaded, or if it can't load, a synthesized
  bone-xylophone tune plays instead, made up as it plays, with its own mood per level (`MUSIC_STYLES`).
- **Ambience**, one quiet sound every few seconds: birds by day, an owl at night, the sleeping long-neck snoring,
  the volcano rumbling, a whoosh as each gust of wind builds, raindrops. There's no constant hiss.
- **Voices** (recorded, see Audio credits): when someone hops in, a passenger voice says where they're going ("Three!
  Fishing!"). The caveman pilot says "Ugh!" when he bumps into something, "Whoa!" when a pterodactyl hits, "Splash!",
  "Yum yum!" when eating, "I'm hungry!" when food runs low, laughs through a smoke ring and shouts "Hooray!" at the
  end. Now and then he says "Ooga booga!" or "Hop in!" on take-off and boarding, passengers say "Whee!" in the air
  and "Yay!" or "Thank you!" when they arrive, a grandma says "Oh my!", and someone who gives up says "Hmph!". One
  line at a time: the stop names always get through, the remarks skip their turn while someone is talking. If a
  recording can't load, the browser's own voice says the words instead.
- 🔊 top right mutes everything (remembered). `?mute` turns audio off completely for testing.

Tuning: `MASTER_VOL`, `MUSIC_VOL`, `MUSIC_DUCK`, `MUSIC_TRACKS` (which loop each mood uses), `MUSIC_XFADE`,
`MUSIC_TRACK_RMS` (the loops' evened-out loudness), `AMBIENCE_VOL`, `MUSIC_STYLES` (the fallback tune per mood),
`VOICE_GAIN` (voices against the effects), `SFX_GAIN` and `SFX_CLIPS` (recorded effects and their levels), `VOICE_GAP`, `REACT_GAP` (seconds between two remarks), `REACT_CHANCE`
(how often the optional remarks are said) and `ASSET_V` (bump after replacing a clip, so phones fetch the new one).

## Vibration

On phones the game buzzes for bumps, landings, hop-ins, deliveries, splashes, smoke rings, low food, someone giving
up and winning (Android vibrates; an iPhone on iOS 18 or later gives system taps, mostly right after a touch). The
📳 button on the level map turns it off and on (remembered); it's hidden where vibration isn't possible.

## Little touches

- The level map shows each level's weather in its corner: ☀️ 🌅 🌙 🌧️ ❄️ 🌋.
- Very cross people let off steam.
- The hint hand taps on levels 1–2 (where a tap flies the copter) and shows "press and hold" later.
